Mini Cybertruck go-kart.

A homemade steel-frame go-kart with electronic steering, remote control, and a custom starter-generator.

From wood to welded steel

The project began with a wooden go-kart built over several summers. Repeated damage exposed the limits of that material and led to a steel-frame version. I sourced scrap steel, worked from CAD designs, and continued the build under a tent in my backyard during the winter.

Making the controls electronic

I built a steering actuator with a high-torque motor, a printed steering horn, and potentiometer feedback. The steering wheel itself used a spring return, with separate triggers for the throttle and brakes. Electronic control also made remote operation possible.

One motor, two jobs

The most interesting mechanism was a motor that could start the engine and then act as a generator. A printed belt transmission and one-way bearings changed the mechanical advantage from 9:1 during starting to 1:1 when the engine drove the motor. Withstanding the engine’s compression torque was a major manufacturing challenge.

A whole system to integrate

The build brought together steering, brake and throttle actuation, power distribution, an engine cutoff, lights, and servo-driven dashboard gauges. The 2024 documentation records a continuing integration effort, rather than a finished commercial vehicle.
